Paris: The Eiffel Tower

Location: Champ de Mars, Paris, France | Completed: 1889

Designed by Gustave Eiffel's engineering firm for the 1889 World's Fair, the Eiffel Tower stands 330 meters tall and remains the most recognizable silhouette in Paris. Originally criticized by several contemporary artists, its wrought-iron latticework soon became a celebrated triumph of modern structural engineering.

Traveler Tip: Visit just before dusk to experience panoramic views across the Seine River during golden hour, then stay at ground level to watch the tower sparkle for five minutes at the top of every hour after sunset.

Rome: The Colosseum

Location: Piazza del Colosseo, Rome, Italy | Completed: 80 AD

Built under the Flavian emperors Vespasian and Titus, the Colosseum is the largest ancient amphitheater ever constructed. Capable of seating over 50,000 spectators, its layered travertine limestone arches and complex underground hypogeum demonstrate the remarkable sophistication of Roman public architecture.

Traveler Tip: Book an early morning combined ticket that includes the Roman Forum and Palatine Hill to beat midday crowds and explore the arena floor while the morning light illuminates the tiered arches.

New York: The Statue of Liberty

Location: Liberty Island, New York Harbor, USA | Dedicated: 1886

Gifted by France to the United States, the Statue of Liberty was sculpted by Frédéric Auguste Bartholdi with an internal iron framework engineered by Gustave Eiffel. Standing in Upper New York Bay, her copper exterior oxidized over decades into its signature sea-green patina, symbolizing freedom and welcoming generations of newcomers.

Traveler Tip: Reserve ferry tickets departing from Battery Park well in advance if you plan to climb the pedestal or crown, and include a stop at nearby Ellis Island on the same ferry loop for the full historical story.
